Getting in

Logging in

Your login page isn't linked anywhere on the site on purpose — bookmark it now.

  1. Go to bkinderknecht.netlify.app/login.html and bookmark it in your browser.
  2. Log in with blainekinderknecht@gmail.com and the password Jack set up for you.
  3. You'll land on the homepage with a thin dark bar pinned to the bottom of the screen that says Editing, plus a round + button floating near it. That bar means you're logged in — the whole site now has edit controls layered on top of the normal page.
Click Log out on that bottom bar when you're done, especially on a shared computer. Nothing about how the site looks changes for anyone else, logged in or not — you're just the only one who sees the edit controls.
The photo library

Adding a photo

Every photo on the site — no matter which category page it shows up on — gets added the same way.

  1. Click the round + button near the bottom bar.
  2. Choose a photo from your computer or phone.
  3. Write a caption (optional, but see the search section below for why it's worth doing), and pick which category it belongs to (Football, Basketball, Farm & Country, etc.).
  4. Click Save.
Every photo you upload is automatically resized for the web — no need to shrink anything yourself first.
The photo library

Editing & deleting one photo

Hover over any photo, anywhere on the site, and two small controls appear on the tile itself.

  1. Edit: click the photo's edit control to change its caption, category, or swap in a different image entirely.
  2. Delete: click the × on the tile to remove it. This can't be undone from the site — if you're not sure, ask Jack before deleting something you might want back.
The photo library

Deleting several photos at once

  1. Click Select in the bottom bar.
  2. Click each photo you want gone — they'll highlight as you go.
  3. Click Delete Selected.
Deleting ten photos this way is one save; deleting them one at a time with the × is ten saves. See the credits section below for why that's worth doing.
The homepage

Your homepage hero photos

The large background photo(s) behind your name at the very top of the site — the first thing anyone sees — are their own thing, separate from the category photos below.

  1. Hover over the hero area at the top of the homepage and click Change hero photos.
  2. Pick up to 3 photos from your library. You can also adjust the framing on your portrait photo from the same panel.
  3. Save.
The photo you pick has to already be uploaded to your library first — add it under the + button before you can choose it here.
Category pages

Category cover photos

Each category (Football, Baseball, Senior Photos, etc.) has its own cover photo shown on the category grid and at the top of that category's page.

  1. Go to that category's page, or find its tile on the "What I shoot" grid on the homepage.
  2. Click Change cover photo / Cover photo.
  3. Choose a photo already in your library, or reset it to blank.
Category pages

Adding & removing categories

  1. Add a category: click + Add Section on the "What I shoot" grid, and name it.
  2. Remove a category: click the × on its tile. Photos already filed under it don't get deleted — only the category itself goes away, so move or re-tag anything in it first if you don't want it orphaned.
  3. Rename a category: click directly into its name text on the grid or on its own page, edit it, then click Save Section Names.
Layout

Reordering photos

Within a category page, click and drag any photo by its ↕ handle to a new spot. Works best on a trackpad.

Every drag is its own save. Moving five photos around one at a time is five separate saves — figure out roughly where you want things before you start dragging, rather than nudging things back and forth repeatedly.
Wording

Page text & bio

Your name, the hero tagline ("Western Kansas & beyond"), your About bio, and the "Book a shoot" heading are all editable directly on the page:

  1. Click directly on any heading or paragraph.
  2. Type your change.
  3. A Save Home Page (or Save About Page) button appears once you've changed something — you can edit several pieces of text before clicking it once.

Your bio can have more paragraphs than it starts with — click into the last line and add another when editing.

The booking page (Book in your nav) has its own editable copy and options, saved the same way via its own Save button.

Contact info

Email, phone, Instagram & TikTok

  1. Click Settings in the bottom bar.
  2. Update any of: email, phone, Instagram link, TikTok link.
  3. Save from inside that panel.
Leave anything blank and it just disappears from the site instead of showing up empty — no need to delete placeholder text, just don't fill it in.
Getting found on Google

Writing for search

Nobody's going to search "photography" and find you — that word is way too competitive. But people searching for exactly what you shoot, in the area you shoot in, absolutely can. The trick isn't stuffing in keywords, it's being specific everywhere you write.

The words that actually matter to you: your sport(s), "high school," and your area (Quinter, Western Kansas) — used the way you'd actually say them out loud, not crammed together.

Quick gut check before you publish anything: if a stranger read only this one sentence, would they know what you shoot and roughly where? If not, that's the fix — not more words, just the right ones.

Photo captions — this is the one that matters most. Every caption doubles as what Google "reads" for that image, so specific captions are genuinely the highest-impact thing you can do here, and they're the easiest.

"Quinter vs. WaKeeney — Senior Night, pole vault"
"State track finals — 4x400 relay, final stretch"
"Nice shot," "Test," "IMG_4821," or leaving it blank

Include what you know: the sport, the teams or schools (if it's public info, like a game result), and the setting or moment. You don't need all three every time — just don't leave it generic.

Your bio already does a lot of this right — it names your area and how long you've been shooting. If you ever revise it, keep your area and what you shoot somewhere in the first couple sentences; that's the part search engines weigh heaviest.

One thing worth doing outside the website entirely: a free Google Business Profile matters more for local searches like "Western Kansas sports photographer" than anything written on the site itself — genuinely the biggest lever for getting found locally. Ask Jack about this if it's not set up yet.
Good to know

When changes go live & how credits work

Every save takes up to about a minute to show up for other visitors — that's the site quietly rebuilding itself in the background, not a bug. You'll always see a confirmation message right after saving; if you don't see one, the change didn't go through and it's worth trying again.

Every save costs a "deploy credit" on the Netlify account — this is real money, not free. As set up, the account is on Netlify's free plan, which is about 20 saves a month. A normal month of editing photos and text won't get close to that, but it's worth knowing the ceiling exists. The way to stay comfortably under it:

  1. Delete photos in batches with Select → Delete Selected instead of one at a time.
  2. Edit several pieces of text before hitting Save. The save button saves everything you've changed on the page at once — no need to save after every single word.
  3. Plan drag-reorders before you start. Each drop is its own save (see above).
  4. Don't save just to "check" something. What you see while editing is already what the site will look like — the save only matters for other visitors.
If it ever runs low mid-month, Netlify will prompt to add credits or upgrade the plan — nothing on the live site breaks either way, new saves just pause until it tops up or the month rolls over. Text Jack if you want a recommendation on which option makes sense.
